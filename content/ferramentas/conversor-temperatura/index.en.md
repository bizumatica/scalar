## The Temperature Scales

Understanding the basis of each scale is essential for scientific applications:

* **Celsius (°C):** Based on the freezing point (0°C) and boiling point (100°C) of water under standard atmospheric pressure.
* **Fahrenheit (°F):** Created by Daniel Fahrenheit, it is still the standard in the US for weather and cooking.
* **Kelvin (K):** The fundamental unit of temperature in the International System of Units (SI). Unlike the others, it does not use "degrees" but is an absolute scale.

## Absolute Zero (0 K)

In physics, temperature is a measurement of molecular motion. **Absolute Zero** is the theoretical state where this motion would stop completely. This occurs at **-273.15°C**. Therefore, the Kelvin scale never has negative values.

### Conversion Formulas

If you need to calculate manually, use the following mathematical relationships:

1.  **Celsius to Fahrenheit:** $$F = (C \times 1.8) + 32$$
2.  **Celsius to Kelvin:** $$K = C + 273.15$$
3.  **Fahrenheit to Celsius:** $$C = (F - 32) \div 1.8$$
